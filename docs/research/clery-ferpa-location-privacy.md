# Clery, FERPA, Title IX, location-privacy and liability constraints on Student incident reporting

**Date:** 2026-11-17 (America/New_York). **Ticket:** [Control707/KSUX#40](https://github.com/Control707/KSUX/issues/40), part of map #35. **Status:** research, **not legal advice**. Each finding carries a confidence label; items marked **Lawyer** or **KSU confirm** need a qualified person before they are relied on.

## Question

Which legal and policy constraints apply to an **unofficial, Student-built** app (KSUX: Next.js PWA, Convex, `.edu`-verified Students, existing Feed, see [SPEC](../SPEC.md)) that lets Students post campus incident observations and shows safety information at Kennesaw State University, a Georgia public university? Specifically: Clery Act, FERPA, location privacy, liability for user posts (Section 230, defamation, Georgia statutes, Open Records) and Title IX.

This builds on the prior campus-safety research instead of repeating it: [campus-safety.md](campus-safety.md) (official categories: advisories vs. timely warnings vs. crime log), [campus-safety-community-feed.md](campus-safety-community-feed.md) (incident Posts in the Feed, consent separation), [campus-safety-handoffs.md](campus-safety-handoffs.md) (LiveSafe/police handoffs, location consent) and [campus-safety-alert-ingestion.md](campus-safety-alert-ingestion.md) (Open Records/reuse note).

## Executive answer

1. **Clery does not bind KSUX directly.** Clery obligations fall on the *institution*; KSUX is not a KSU department, and an ordinary Student maintainer is not a Campus Security Authority (CSA). A KSUX Post creates **no** KSU reporting, timely-warning or crime-log obligation, because those are triggered only by reports to KSU Police, local police or a CSA. The risk is the *opposite*: a Student may believe posting = reporting. KSUX must say plainly that a Post is **not** a report to KSU. Two traps would change the analysis: (a) KSU listing KSUX as a place to report crimes (that alone makes an organization a CSA), and (b) a maintainer who is personally a CSA (e.g. an RA or a student-org advisor). *Confidence: high on the regulatory text; KSU confirm on (b).*
2. **FERPA does not apply to KSUX today.** FERPA covers records *maintained by an educational institution or a party acting for it*. Student-authored content held by an independent Student project is not an education record. FERPA could attach if KSU **adopted, contracted, or directed** KSUX (the "school official" outsourcing route) or fed it KSU education-record data. *Confidence: high; Lawyer/KSU confirm before any formal KSU partnership.*
3. **Location: no Georgia-specific statute was found that forbids it, but app-store rules, browser permission, the FTC Act and KSU's own guidance converge on the same default:** collect location only on explicit user action, never publish precise coordinates or reporter location, publish only coarse incident location ("general location" mirrors the Clery crime-log standard), keep it short-lived, never sell/share it. *Confidence: medium-high (state-law absence is unverified).*
4. **User posts are largely shielded by Section 230**, but not KSUX's *own* words: labels, headlines, moderator edits, and especially **`Scrappy` AI summaries** of incident Posts may be treated as KSUX-created content. Posters remain personally liable for libel (O.C.G.A. § 51-5-1) and for false reports *to police* (§ 16-10-26). Accusations naming/identifying people are the main defamation risk. *Confidence: high on statute text; Lawyer on Scrappy.*
5. **Georgia Open Records does not reach KSUX's data** as an independent Student project; it would if KSUX performed a function **for or on behalf of** KSU (O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70(b)(2)). *Confidence: medium-high; Lawyer/KSU confirm.*
6. **Title IX: KSUX has no Title IX duty, but a public feed is a poor and potentially harmful channel for sexual-misconduct reports.** A Post does not give KSU "actual knowledge" (that needs the Title IX Coordinator or an official with corrective authority), it can't give the victim the confidentiality Clery/VAWA require of KSU, and it can expose and re-identify victims. KSUX should divert, not host, such reports. *Confidence: high on regulatory text; KSU confirm on current KSU policy wording.*

## Findings

### 1. Clery Act (20 U.S.C. § 1092(f); 34 CFR § 668.46)

- **Who is a CSA.** The regulation defines four kinds: (i) a campus police/security department; (ii) individuals with campus-security responsibility; (iii) "Any individual or organization **specified in an institution's statement of campus security policy** as an individual or organization to which students and employees should report criminal offenses"; (iv) officials with significant responsibility for student and campus activities (housing, discipline, judicial). [34 CFR § 668.46(a), "Campus security authority"](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-668.46). KSUX is none of these unless KSU names it in its Annual Security Report (ASR) under (iii). *High.*
- **KSU's own CSA list** names "the Dean of Students, the Title IX Coordinator, student staff in residence life, athletic coaches, and advisors to student organizations, to include some faculty members," and says crimes reported to them are shared with University Police "for the purposes of investigation, Timely Warnings and inclusion in the Annual Security Report." [KSU 2026 ASFR, p. 9](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/offices-services/public-safety-police/docs/final-2026-asfr.pdf). So a KSUX maintainer who is **also** an RA or a student-org advisor is personally a CSA in that role. Whether reading a KSUX moderation queue counts as receiving a report "in their CSA capacity" is **unverified** → **KSU confirm** with the KSU Clery compliance office. *Medium.*
- **Trigger for statistics, timely warnings and crime log is a report to campus police, local police, or a CSA.** Statistics count crimes "reported to local police agencies or to a campus security authority" (§ 668.46(c)(1)); timely warnings cover crimes "Reported to campus security authorities ... or local police agencies" and "Considered by the institution to represent a threat" (§ 668.46(e)(1)); the crime log records crimes "reported to the campus police or the campus security department" (§ 668.46(f)(1)). [34 CFR § 668.46](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-668.46). A KSUX Post reaches none of these. Therefore a Post **does not** start a Clery count, warning decision or log entry. *High.*
- **Implication for UX.** Because nothing official happens, KSUX must not imply that it does. KSU publishes the real reporting routes (University Police `470-578-6666`, non-emergency `470-578-6206`, anonymous reporting `470-578-6206`, text line, LiveSafe) and states LiveSafe lets users share information with KSU officials anonymously. [ASFR pp. 6–8, 11](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/offices-services/public-safety-police/docs/final-2026-asfr.pdf). Handoff design is already covered in [campus-safety-handoffs.md](campus-safety-handoffs.md).
- **What KSUX must not imply about Clery:** that a Post is a crime report, a "Clery report," a timely warning or an emergency notification; that no official warning means "safe" (already a finding in [campus-safety.md §2](campus-safety.md)); that KSUX is affiliated with KSU Police; or that KSUX data are KSU crime statistics.
- **Useful Clery norms KSUX can borrow (not required of it):** timely warnings withhold victim names/identifying information (§ 668.46(e)(1); ASFR p. 13); the crime log records only "nature, date, time, and **general location**" (§ 668.46(f)(1)(i); ASFR p. 48) and may withhold details that would jeopardize an investigation or someone's safety (§ 668.46(f)(3)). These are good, defensible templates for KSUX Posts. *High (as norms).*

### 2. FERPA (20 U.S.C. § 1232g; 34 CFR Part 99)

- **Education records** are records "(1) Directly related to a student; and (2) Maintained by an educational agency or institution **or by a party acting for the agency or institution**." [34 CFR § 99.3](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.3). KSUX's Convex data are maintained by Student maintainers acting for themselves, not for KSU, so they are not education records and FERPA imposes no duty on KSUX. *High.*
- **When FERPA could attach.** KSU may treat an outside party as a "school official" only if it "Performs an institutional service or function for which the agency or institution would otherwise use employees," is "under the direct control of the agency or institution with respect to the use and maintenance of education records," and is bound by § 99.33(a) redisclosure limits. [34 CFR § 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.31). Triggers for KSUX: a contract/MOU, KSU-directed operation, receipt of KSU education-record data (e.g. a roster, enrollment, conduct records), or official adoption as a KSU reporting channel. Mere endorsement or a link is **probably** not enough, but that line needs **Lawyer/KSU confirm**. *Medium.*
- **Police records are separately carved out.** Records created and maintained by a law-enforcement unit for a law-enforcement purpose are not education records ([34 CFR §§ 99.3, 99.8](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-99.8)), which is why KSU can publish its crime log. This does not make KSUX content "police records." *High.*
- **SPEC already avoids the main FERPA-adjacent data:** `.ics` schedule import is Student-supplied and `courseSections` come from public catalogs ([SPEC §5](../SPEC.md)). Keep it that way: never ingest KSU-internal Student records.

### 3. Location privacy

- **Browser.** The W3C Geolocation API requires a secure context and express permission, and does not guarantee accuracy; a permission grant is not consent to publish or forward (prior finding, [campus-safety-community-feed.md](campus-safety-community-feed.md); [W3C Geolocation](https://www.w3.org/TR/2026/CRD-geolocation-20261117/)). *High.*
- **App-store policies (relevant if KSUX ships a wrapped app later).** Apple 5.1.5: "Use Location Services in your app only when it is directly relevant to the features and services provided by the app. Location-based APIs shouldn't be used to provide emergency services ... Ensure that you notify and obtain consent before collecting, transmitting, or using location data." [App Review Guidelines 5.1.5](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#location). Apple 5.1.1(i) also requires permission and disclosure before sharing personal data. Google Play restricts background location to features with significant user benefit and requires prominent disclosure ([Play Console Help, background location](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9799150)). A PWA on Vercel is not store-reviewed, but these are the norms the spec should meet anyway. *High (text); the "no emergency services" line is a further reason not to present KSUX as an emergency service.*
- **FTC.** Section 5 of the FTC Act prohibits "unfair or deceptive acts or practices in or affecting commerce" ([15 U.S.C. § 45](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/45)); the FTC treats precise location as sensitive, e.g. its first data-broker order banning sale of sensitive location data ([FTC, X-Mode/Outlogic, Jan 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/01/ftc-order-prohibits-data-broker-x-mode-social-outlogic-selling-sensitive-location-data), read via text relay because ftc.gov blocked the fetcher). Whether a free, non-commercial Student project is "in commerce" for FTC purposes is **unverified/Lawyer**; the practical rule is the same: say exactly what you do with location and do it. *Medium.*
- **Georgia.** No Georgia statute specifically regulating app collection or display of location was found in this limited search; Georgia did not appear to have a comprehensive consumer privacy act at retrieval time. **Unverified**; this is a negative search result, not a finding of absence → **Lawyer**.
- **Minors on campus.** COPPA applies to children "under the age of 13" ([16 CFR § 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/section-312.2)) and lists geolocation "sufficient to identify street name and name of a city or town" as personal information. KSUX's `.edu`-only Students are very unlikely to be under 13, so COPPA is a low risk, but KSU does enroll some under-18 dual-enrollment students and has a "Protecting Minors" policy area (linked from KSU site navigation; not reviewed). Treat minors as a reason for stricter defaults, not a separate regime. *Medium; KSU confirm on minors.*
- **KSU's own safety advice** warns: "Be careful about posting your location ... Consider disabling this function." [ASFR p. 46](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/offices-services/public-safety-police/docs/final-2026-asfr.pdf). A KSUX that publicly places reporters would cut against KSU's own guidance.
- **Retention.** No statute found that sets a retention period for KSUX. SPEC already strips EXIF/GPS from images and uses short decay windows and hourly purges ([SPEC §2, §5](../SPEC.md)); apply the same to any incident location field. *Prudent default, not legal requirement.*

### 4. Liability for user posts

- **Section 230.** "No provider or user of an interactive computer service shall be treated as the publisher or speaker of any information provided by another information content provider," and good-faith moderation is protected; inconsistent state-law claims are preempted. [47 U.S.C. § 230(c), (e)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/47/230). A Student's defamatory incident Post is generally the poster's speech, not KSUX's. *High.*
- **Limits that matter for KSUX:** § 230 does not cover content KSUX *itself* creates or materially develops. Candidates: incident headlines/badges that assert facts ("Assault reported at X"), moderator rewrites, auto-generated categories, and **`Scrappy` LLM summaries of incident Posts** (SPEC §6.4). Whether AI-generated summaries of third-party content are protected is unsettled → **Lawyer**. § 230 also does not bar federal criminal law or intellectual-property claims (§ 230(e)(1)–(2)). *Medium.*
- **Defamation (Georgia).** "A libel is a false and malicious defamation of another, expressed in print, writing, pictures, or signs, tending to injure the reputation of the person ... The publication of the libelous matter is essential to recovery." [O.C.G.A. § 51-5-1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-51-torts/ga-code-sect-51-5-1/) (FindLaw copy, current as of 2024-03-28; verify against the official code). The highest-risk Posts identify a named or recognizable person (name, photo, handle, unique description) as a criminal. The poster bears that risk; KSUX reduces it (and the harm to the person) by prohibiting identification. *High (text), Lawyer (application).*
- **False reports.** "A person who willfully and knowingly gives or causes a false report of a crime to be given to any law enforcement officer or agency of this state is guilty of a misdemeanor." [O.C.G.A. § 16-10-26](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-16-crimes-and-offenses/ga-code-sect-16-10-26/). This applies to reports **to law enforcement**, not to a Feed Post itself, which is one more reason to keep "Post" and "contact police" separate in UX. A related false-public-alarm offense (O.C.G.A. § 16-11-37 terroristic threats; § 16-10-28 false public alarm) was **not retrieved** → unverified.
- **Harassment.** Georgia criminalizes repeated electronic contact "for the purpose of harassing, molesting, threatening, or intimidating," and threats of bodily harm, and excludes constitutionally protected speech. [O.C.G.A. § 16-11-39.1](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-16-crimes-and-offenses/ga-code-sect-16-11-39-1/). Relevant to brigading and pile-ons in incident threads. *High (text).*
- **Georgia Open Records.** "Public record" covers material "prepared and maintained or received by an agency **or by a private person or entity in the performance of a service or function for or on behalf of an agency**." [O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70(b)(2)](https://codes.findlaw.com/ga/title-50-state-government/ga-code-sect-50-18-70/). An independent KSUX is outside this; a KSU-commissioned or KSU-operated KSUX could bring its incident data (including reporter identity and location) within reach of records requests. Prior research noted KSU's privacy notice references the Open Records Act ([campus-safety-alert-ingestion.md](campus-safety-alert-ingestion.md)). *Medium-high; Lawyer/KSU confirm.*
- **Subpoenas.** Any data KSUX stores (handles, canonical emails, IP hashes, location) can be subpoenaed in a defamation or criminal matter regardless of § 230. Data minimization is the only real protection. *High (general principle); specifics Lawyer.*

### 5. Title IX (20 U.S.C. § 1681; 34 CFR Part 106)

- **Which rule.** The eCFR text current at retrieval is the 2020 rule (definitions of "actual knowledge," "deliberately indifferent" response). [34 CFR § 106.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-106.30), [§ 106.44(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-106.44). The status of the 2024 amendments (litigated) was not independently confirmed from an ED page (ed.gov blocked the fetcher) → **unverified**; the conclusions below hold under either rule because KSUX is not a recipient.
- **Duty sits with KSU, triggered by notice to specific people.** Actual knowledge means notice "to a recipient's Title IX Coordinator or any official of the recipient who has authority to institute corrective measures"; a mere "ability or obligation to report" does not qualify. [§ 106.30](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-106.30). Any person may report to the Coordinator by any means that "results in the Title IX Coordinator receiving the person's verbal or written report" ([§ 106.8(a)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-106.8)). A public KSUX Post is **not** a Title IX report and does not put KSU on notice unless an official happens to read it, which KSUX cannot control or promise. *High.*
- **Victim confidentiality.** KSU must keep victims' identifying information out of public Clery records and keep accommodations confidential ([§ 668.46(b)(11)(iii)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/section-668.46)); KSU's ASFR describes Confidential/Privileged employees (counseling, SAFE Center, health clinic) vs. Responsible employees, anonymous reporting, and that complainants may request their identity be withheld ([ASFR p. 39](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/offices-services/public-safety-police/docs/final-2026-asfr.pdf)). KSU's Title IX page points to the Office of Institutional Equity, the Title IX Coordinator, confidential resources and "Report a Concern" ([KSU Title IX](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/offices-services/institutional-equity/title-ix/index.php)). A public feed provides none of this and can re-identify victims through time, place and detail even without names.
- **Collision.** Yes: a public incident feed collides with sexual-misconduct reporting in three ways: (1) false sense of having reported, (2) loss of victim control over disclosure (screenshots persist after deletion), (3) third-party posts about someone else's assault or naming an alleged perpetrator (defamation plus retraumatization). Dating violence, domestic violence and stalking are Clery crimes with the same confidentiality concerns (§ 668.46(a)). *High.*

## Done when: hard constraints vs. prudent defaults for the spec

"Hard" = required by a cited statute/regulation/platform rule, or by the factual impossibility of KSUX doing what a claim would imply. "Prudent" = strongly recommended, not legally required as far as this research found.

### Hard constraints

1. **H1 — No official-report claims.** No UI/copy may state or imply that a Post is a report to KSU, KSU Police, a CSA, the Title IX Coordinator, a "Clery report," a timely warning or an emergency notification (Clery triggers are reports to police/CSAs only, § 668.46(c)(1), (e)(1), (f)(1); Title IX notice requires the Coordinator/authorized official, § 106.30). Pair every incident composer with the official routes (KSU Police / 911 / LiveSafe / Title IX), as the existing `Campus Safety` report callout already does ([SPEC §4.5](../SPEC.md)).
2. **H2 — No KSU affiliation claims.** No "official," KSU marks or police endorsement implied (also reinforces SPEC's reserved handles `ksupd`, `police`, `official`). Avoids becoming, or appearing to be, an ASR-listed reporting organization (CSA via § 668.46(a)(iii)) and avoids deceptive-practice risk (15 U.S.C. § 45).
3. **H3 — Location only with explicit, purpose-specific consent; disclose exactly what is collected and shown.** Browser permission is required by the platform; consent-before-collection/transmission is required by Apple 5.1.5/5.1.1 if ever store-distributed; misdescribing data practices is the FTC § 5 risk.
4. **H4 — Do not present KSUX as an emergency service** (Apple 5.1.5 bars location APIs for emergency services; KSUX cannot dispatch). Emergency paths hand off to 911/KSU Police.
5. **H5 — No ingestion of KSU education records** (rosters, conduct, grades, non-public directory data) without a formal agreement; doing so is the path by which FERPA (§ 99.31(a)(1)(i)(B)) and Georgia Open Records (§ 50-18-70(b)(2)) could attach. Any future KSU adoption/contract triggers a **Lawyer/KSU review gate** before launch.
6. **H6 — Honor takedown of KSUX-authored content.** Section 230 does not protect KSUX's own text; any KSUX-generated label, headline, moderator edit or `Scrappy` summary about an incident is KSUX speech and must be correctable/removable on notice.

### Prudent defaults

1. **P1 — Coarse public location only:** Campus + building/area or landmark, no coordinates, no reporter's current location, no residence-hall room numbers (mirrors the Clery crime log's "general location", § 668.46(f)(1)(i)). Incident location is chosen by the Student, never inferred from GPS or the Campus filter (prior finding).
2. **P2 — No identification of people in incident Posts:** no names, handles, faces, plates, room numbers, or unique descriptions of victims **or** alleged perpetrators; extend the existing write-time guardrails to these patterns. Allow physical-description fields only for a currently active threat, and prefer linking to the official alert instead.
3. **P3 — Sexual misconduct, dating/domestic violence and stalking are diverted, not published:** selecting these categories shows KSU Title IX/SAFE Center/confidential resources, KSU Police and LiveSafe (anonymous option) and does **not** offer a public Post by default. If a Student still writes one, hold it for review and strip identifying detail.
4. **P4 — Clear "not a report" label on every incident Post and composer**, e.g. "Community post — not reported to KSU Police. To report: [Call] [LiveSafe]."
5. **P5 — Short retention:** incident Posts decay from the Feed on a short window (in line with SPEC's read-time decay pattern) and incident location fields are purged on a schedule (e.g. ≤30 days, matching existing purges); never log precise coordinates server-side. No location in push payloads (SPEC already uses handle-only payloads).
6. **P6 — Strip image metadata** (already in SPEC: client-side WebP re-encode strips EXIF/GPS) and warn before posting photos of people.
7. **P7 — `Scrappy` excluded from incident content:** do not let `Scrappy` summarize, rank or re-state incident Posts (H6 + unsettled AI-summary § 230 status), or at minimum cite the original Post verbatim with its "community, not official" label.
8. **P8 — Faster quarantine for incident Posts:** fewer than the general 3-report threshold to auto-hide when flagged for identifying a person or being false; a "this identifies me" removal path.
9. **P9 — Minimal reporter data:** store only what SPEC already stores for any Post; no extra reporter identity or device data for incidents. This limits subpoena exposure and any future Open Records exposure.
10. **P10 — Terms/Community Guidelines:** a short statement that posters are responsible for their content, that false accusations may be unlawful, that KSUX is unofficial, and how to request removal. Maintainers who are CSAs (RA, student-org advisor) should not personally run the moderation queue for incident content until **KSU confirms** the CSA question.
